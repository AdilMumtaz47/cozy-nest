# 30 Boho Bedroom Ideas for a Calm, Layered Retreat

A boho bedroom should feel like the calmest room in the house — soft linen, warm wood, woven textures, and plants that make the air feel alive. It is the bedroom version of the style that works so well in living rooms: nothing matches exactly, everything belongs, and the room looks gathered over time rather than bought in a weekend. This list gathers 30 real bedrooms, from pale macrame rooms to deep green jungle corners, so you can steal one idea or build the whole look.

The rule that holds every room together is simple: start with a warm neutral base, let the bed carry the texture, and add plants at three heights. If you love this layered warmth in the living room too, our [30 Boho Living Room Ideas for a Relaxed, Layered Home](/posts/boho-living-room/) shows the same materials at a bigger scale, and [30 Neutral Bedroom Ideas for a Calm, Timeless Retreat](/posts/neutral-bedroom/) is the quieter cousin of this list.

## 1. A Macrame Wall Hanging Over a Light Wood Bed

One large macrame hanging can carry the whole wall. Here it hangs above a light wood bed dressed in white linen, with a jute rug underfoot and plants on the windowsill and the nightstand. The woven bench at the foot of the bed, with a straw bag and a fringed throw, finishes the corner.

Keep the bedding plain white so the macrame is the pattern in the room. The trick is scale — the hanging is wide enough to span most of the bed, so the wall needs nothing else.

## 2. Layered Macrame and Plants by the Bed

Layering is what makes boho feel collected. This bedroom hangs a large macrame piece above the bed, flanked by smaller hangings, dreamcatchers and trailing plants, with a chunky knit throw over grey linen bedding. A rattan lamp on the nightstand glows warm in the evening.

The plants are doing as much work as the macrame — a fiddle leaf fig in one corner, pothos trailing from the wall, and a floor cushion for reading. If your wall feels bare, add plants before you add more art.

## 3. A Large Macrame Tapestry Over Neutral Linens

When the macrame is this intricate, the rest of the room stays quiet. A large tapestry with a diamond pattern hangs above a wooden bed dressed in cream and beige linens, with a woven throw across the foot. A single plant and a candle on the nightstand are the only other decoration.

This is the look to copy if you love boho but not clutter: one hero textile, neutral bedding, and natural wood. The room feels calm because nothing is competing for attention.

## 4. Cream Macrame with Mustard Accent Pillows

Mustard is the color that wakes up a neutral boho bedroom. Here a cream macrame hanging with long fringe sits above a bed dressed in white linen, with mustard pillows and a beige throw. Hanging plants in macrame holders frame the bed on both sides.

Keep the mustard to the pillows and one throw — a little goes a long way against all that cream. The jute rug under the bed ties the warm tones together and softens the wood floor.

## 5. A Macrame Headboard Wall with Patterned Pillows

Treat the wall above the bed as the headboard. This room hangs a tall macrame piece between two rope details, with patterned pillows in rust, teal and cream doing the color work on white bedding. A woven pouf at the foot of the bed adds a low seat.

The pillows are the only strong pattern in the room, so they get to be bold. If your bedding is plain, the pillows are where the personality lives — mix two patterns and one solid in the same color family.

## 6. A Round Macrame with Hanging Pendant Lights

Round shapes soften a bedroom full of straight lines. A round macrame medallion hangs above the bed, flanked by small rattan pendant lights on long cords, with a wooden bench at the foot of the bed. The bedding stays cream and beige, so the shapes do the talking.

Hang the pendants at different heights for a collected feel, and keep them on dimmers — rattan shades throw the prettiest patterned light in the evening, and that glow is half the point.

## 7. A Macrame Corner with a White Vanity

A bedroom corner can hold a whole ritual. This one pairs a large macrame hanging with a small white vanity, a rattan stool, a round mirror and woven wall discs, beside a bed dressed in white and peach. A plant in a woven basket fills the floor space.

The vanity is the clever part — it gives the macrame a job to frame, instead of hanging alone on a blank wall. If you have an awkward corner, a small table and a hanging above it will claim it.

## 8. Woven Wall Decor with a Terracotta Throw

Woven discs, macrame and a patterned wall hanging can share one wall if they share one palette. Here they hang above a wooden bed dressed in white linen with a terracotta throw, under a big rattan pendant light. A woven pouf and a round jute rug finish the floor.

The terracotta throw is the only strong color, and it repeats in the wall hanging. One color, repeated in textiles, is what makes a busy wall feel intentional instead of random.

## 9. A Rattan Headboard with Orange Pillows

A curved rattan headboard is the fastest way to make a bedroom feel boho. This one arches over a bed dressed in cream linen with orange pillows and a beige throw, beside a rattan chair and a round wooden side table with a clay vase. A woven pendant light hangs above.

The orange pillows are doing the color work — without them the room would be all cream and wood. Pick one warm accent color and repeat it in the pillows, the throw and one small object, and the room will feel finished.

## 10. A Fan-Shaped Rattan Headboard with Pink Bedding

A fan-shaped rattan headboard turns the bed into the room's sculpture. Here it arches over blush pink bedding with a dusty pink throw, flanked by plants and a woven side table. Round rattan mirrors and a small macrame hanging fill the wall without competing.

Pink works in boho when it is dusty rather than bright — blush, terracotta and clay all sit comfortably with rattan and jute. Keep the rug neutral so the pink has room to breathe.

## 11. A Cane Bed with Plants and Floor Cushions

Cane and rattan make a bedroom feel like a greenhouse in the best way. This bright room centers on a cane bed with white linen, surrounded by a fiddle leaf fig, trailing plants and floor cushions in kilim patterns. A round jute rug and a woven basket of blankets fill the floor.

The plants are at three heights — tall in the corner, trailing from the shelf, small on the nightstand — so the greenery feels like part of the room's architecture. Water them on the same day each week and they will stay the easiest decor you own.

## 12. Rattan Furniture with Hanging Basket Lights

A full rattan set can look like a showroom, but this room avoids it. The bed, the cabinet and the hanging baskets are all different weaves, the bedding is plain white, and a woven pouf and a round rug break up the floor. The wall discs above the bed are all different sizes.

The lesson is to mix the weave: rattan, seagrass, jute and cane in the same room, but never the same pattern twice. One orange cushion is the only strong color, and it is enough.

## 13. A Woven Bed Frame with Layered Bedding

A woven bed frame is a statement that needs little else. This sunlit room pairs it with layered cream bedding, a chunky knit throw, woven wall discs and a hanging plant, with sunlight streaming across the floor. The rug is a simple jute, so the bed frame stays the star.

Keep the bedding in one color family — cream, beige, sand — and let the texture do the work. Layered bedding in one tone reads as calm; the same layers in four colors would read as busy.

## 14. Rattan Accents with Wicker Pendant Lights

Wicker pendant lights are the boho bedroom's best evening feature. Here two large woven pendants hang over a rattan bed dressed in white linen, with a round jute rug and a woven pouf at the foot. Plants fill the corners and the windowsill.

Hang the pendants low enough to glow over the bed but high enough to sit up under — about seven feet from the floor is the sweet spot. On a dimmer, they turn the whole room golden at night.

## 15. A Rattan Bed with a Woven Trunk at the Foot

A trunk at the foot of the bed is storage that looks like decor. This bright room pairs a rattan bed with white linen and a woven trunk dressed with a throw, beside a rattan nightstand and a round woven wall disc. The rug is a pale jute, so the room stays light.

Use the trunk for spare blankets and out-of-season bedding — it earns its floor space. A throw draped over it softens the hard lines and ties it to the bed.

## 16. A Rattan Bed with a Jute Rug and Peacock Chair

A peacock chair is the boho accent that needs no introduction. This room pairs a rattan bed with white linen and a round jute rug, and puts a rattan peacock chair with a teal cushion in the corner. Woven wall discs and rattan pendant lights fill the wall above the bed.

Give the chair a cushion and a throw so it gets used, not just looked at. A chair that nobody sits in becomes clutter; a chair with a cushion becomes the reading spot.

## 17. A Curved Rattan Headboard with a Sun Mirror

A sunburst mirror is the jewelry of a boho bedroom. Here it hangs above a curved rattan headboard dressed in white linen with a grey throw, beside a tall plant and a rattan bench. The woven wall discs and the round mirror keep the wall collected.

Mirrors do double duty in a bedroom — they bounce light and they make the room feel bigger. Hang the sun mirror where it catches the window light, and the whole wall will glow in the morning.

## 18. A Plant-Filled Room with Sage Green Bedding

Sage green is boho's calmest color. This room dresses the bed in layered sage and olive bedding with textured pillows, hangs macrame from the ceiling beams, and fills the windowsill and the floor with plants. A woven pouf and a vintage rug finish the floor.

The green reads as a plant color, so the room's real plants feel like part of the palette rather than decoration. If you want color without pattern, pick one muted green and let the textiles match it.

## 19. A Wooden Bed with Layered Bedding and Plants

Layered bedding is the heart of a boho bedroom. This room dresses a wooden bed in cream, rust and olive layers — a quilt, a throw, a stack of pillows — and surrounds it with potted plants on the floor, the nightstand and a vintage rug. A dreamcatcher hangs on the wall.

The trick is to layer in one color family: cream, rust and olive all sit close together, so the bed can carry five layers without looking busy. Fold the throw across the foot, not the middle, so the bed still looks made.

## 20. A Dark Boho Bedroom with Green Bedding

Dark walls make a bedroom feel like a cocoon. This one paints the walls charcoal, dresses the bed in sage and forest green bedding, and fills the corners with tall plants. A woven wall hanging and a basket on the floor keep the natural materials in play.

Dark walls need warm light — a rattan pendant, a bedside lamp, candles — or the room will feel cold. With the light right, a dark boho bedroom is the coziest room in the house.

## 21. A Terracotta Wall with Dreamcatchers and Macrame

One painted wall can set the whole palette. This room paints the wall behind the bed terracotta, hangs dreamcatchers and macrame on it, and dresses the bed in cream, rust and olive bedding. A wooden bench at the foot holds a candle and a book.

The terracotta wall is doing the work of a headboard, a color scheme and a backdrop at once. If you rent, a large terracotta tapestry gives you the same effect without the paint.

## 22. A Macrame Wall with Lush Plants and Warm Light

Golden hour is a decorating tool. This room hangs a macrame piece on a textured wall, fills the corner with tall plants, and lets the low sun wash across the bed dressed in cream and rust layers. A vintage rug in faded reds grounds the floor.

The light is the point here — sheer curtains, not blackout, so the sun comes through warm. If your bedroom gets evening sun, keep the walls pale and let the light be the color.

## 23. Sage Green Walls with Floral Bedding

Sage walls and floral bedding are a gentle combination. This room paints the walls soft sage, dresses the bed in green floral bedding with a mint throw, and hangs a small macrame piece and a rattan pendant light. Plants on the windowsill and the shelf bring the outside in.

Floral bedding works in boho when the flowers are faded and botanical rather than bright and graphic. Keep the throw plain so the pattern has room, and the room will feel like a garden, not a wallpaper sample.

## 24. A Plant-Filled Room with Olive Bedding

This is the jungle version of boho. A bed dressed in olive green bedding with an orange knit throw sits under a wall of hanging plants, with monstera leaves framing the windows and a patterned rug underfoot. Every surface holds a plant.

The orange throw is the only warm color, and it is what keeps the green from feeling cold. One warm accent in a green room is the difference between a greenhouse and a bedroom.

## 25. A Chunky Knit Throw with Botanical Wall Art

A chunky knit throw is the coziest layer a bed can wear. This bright room pairs one with cream linen bedding, botanical prints in thin frames, hanging plants and a woven pouf. The dreamcatcher and macrame on the wall keep the boho notes without overwhelming the room.

Knit throws work best folded across the foot of the bed, where they invite you to pull them up. Choose one in cream or oatmeal so it goes with every season's bedding.

## 26. A Deep Green Wall with Layered Green Bedding

Deep green walls make white bedding glow. This room paints the wall behind the bed a rich sage green, dresses the bed in layered green and cream bedding with a fringed throw, and adds a plant on the nightstand and a hanging bulb. The room feels calm and a little dramatic at once.

One dark wall is enough — paint only the wall behind the bed and keep the other three pale. The bed becomes the focal point, and the room still feels bright in the morning.

## 27. Green and Mustard Room with a Woven Tapestry

Green and mustard are boho's warmest pairing. This room paints the walls deep green, hangs a woven tapestry above the bed, and dresses the bed in green bedding with a mustard throw and pillows. A striped rug in green, cream and mustard ties the floor to the bed.

The rug is doing quiet work here — it repeats both colors, so the room feels planned. If you paint the walls green, let the rug carry the second color and the bedding will follow.

## 28. A Dark Jungle Green Bedroom with a Lantern

This is boho at its moodiest. Deep green curtains cover the wall behind the bed, the bedding is dark green and gold, and a glowing lantern sits on the floor beside a furry floor cushion. Plants frame the windows and the light is low and warm.

The lantern is the key — in a dark room, one warm light source makes everything feel intentional. Use a real candle lantern or a warm LED one, and keep the bedding textured so it catches the light.

## 29. Throw Blankets with Plants and Macrame

Throw blankets are the easiest boho layer to add. This room drapes a chunky knit throw and a rust throw over cream bedding, hangs macrame on the wall, and fills the windowsill with plants. A woven pouf and a patterned rug finish the floor.

Keep two throws on the bed — one folded at the foot, one draped over the corner — so the bed looks lived in rather than staged. Different textures, same color family, and the bed will look inviting every time you walk in.

## 30. A Sunlit Room with Layered Textiles and Greenery

End the list with the sunniest room in it. A bed dressed in layered cream and beige textiles sits under a wall of hanging plants and shelves, with sunlight streaming through the window and a patterned rug underfoot. A wooden bench at the foot holds a woven basket.

The shelves are the clever part — they hold plants at eye level, so the greenery surrounds the bed instead of sitting on the floor. If you have a blank wall, a shelf of plants is the fastest boho transformation there is.

## Takeaway

A boho bedroom is not a shopping list — it is a way of layering. Start with a warm neutral base, anchor the bed with a natural-fiber rug, hang one good macrame piece above the bed, then add plants at three heights and textiles one at a time. Edit as often as you add, and let the room collect slowly. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What makes a bedroom feel boho?

A boho bedroom feels gathered, not bought in one go. Mix natural materials like rattan, jute, linen and raw wood, layer textiles in two or three patterns, and add plants at scale. Keep the base calm — warm white walls and neutral bedding — then let the macrame, cushions and handmade pieces carry the color and the personality.

### What colors work best in a boho bedroom?

Warm earth tones do the heavy lifting: terracotta, rust, ochre, sand, olive and cream sit close together, so a room can carry several without looking busy. Keep walls and the largest bedding neutral, then bring color through the pillows and throws. One deeper accent — sage green, mustard or burgundy — adds depth without breaking the calm.

### How do I hang macrame in a bedroom?

Hang one large piece centered above the bed, with its bottom edge about six to eight inches above the headboard. Use a proper wall hook rated for its weight, not a stick-on hook. If the piece is heavy, anchor it into a stud. Smaller hangings work in pairs beside the bed or above a vanity.

### What bedding suits a boho bedroom?

Natural fibers in soft, washed textures: linen, washed cotton or a cotton-linen blend in cream, oatmeal or white. Layer a duvet, a quilt or coverlet, and a throw in the same color family, with two or three pillows in different textures. Avoid shiny sateen or stiff percale — boho bedding should look soft and slightly rumpled.

### How many plants should a boho bedroom have?

Enough to feel lush, not so many the room becomes a greenhouse. Three plants at three heights in one corner — a tall fiddle leaf fig, a trailing pothos and a small pot at the base — reads as intentional. Repeat it once more across the room, choose plants that suit your light, and keep the pots in natural materials.

### Can a boho bedroom work in a small room?

Yes — boho suits small rooms because it is built on layers, not bulk. Use a round jute rug to define the bed area, one rattan chair instead of a second seat, and hang plants and macrame on the walls to free the floor. Keep the palette light and the furniture low so the room reads bigger than it is.

### What lighting works best in a boho bedroom?

Light from three low sources at 2700 Kelvin, never from the ceiling alone. A woven rattan pendant for general light, a bedside lamp for reading, and candles or fairy lights for evenings. Warm light is what makes the textures — jute, rattan, linen — glow instead of looking flat.

### How do I keep a boho bedroom from looking cluttered?

Edit as often as you add. A bohemian room without breathing room is clutter with a rattan chair in it. Leave some shelves and surfaces empty, limit the room to two or three patterns with one clearly in charge, and give every small object a group or a tray. If a piece has no story or no job, it goes.
