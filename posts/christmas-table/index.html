30 Christmas Table Ideas for a Festive Dinner

By Cozy Nest · Nov 23, 2026 · 20 min read

Save this list on Pinterest

A christmas table is where the whole day gathers — the meal, the candles, the conversation that runs long after the plates are cleared. These 30 christmas table ideas cover every style, from a classic red and gold tablescape to a quiet Scandinavian setting, so you can dress the table you already have without buying a whole new set of dishes.

Each idea below is a real table, photographed as it was set: a greenery garland down the middle, taper candles in brass holders, plaid runners, woven placemats, name cards and napkin rings that take two minutes each. Pick one look for Christmas dinner, or mix a runner from one table with the place settings from another — the pieces are simple and most of them come from things you already own. If you are dressing the whole house too, our cozy christmas ideas carry the same palette from the mantel to the front door.

1. A Classic Red, Green and Gold Centerpiece

Start with the look everyone pictures when they hear "Christmas dinner." A low evergreen centerpiece runs down the middle of the table, packed with pinecones, red berries, gold baubles and a red ribbon bow, with pillar candles rising out of the greenery. It is the one centerpiece that works on any table, in any dining room, with any plates underneath it.

Keep the candles in the middle and the plates simple — white or cream lets the greenery do the talking. A burlap or linen runner under the centerpiece protects the table and gives the whole setting a finished edge without a full tablecloth.

02 of 30

2. A Tartan Plaid Table for the Whole Family

A tartan plaid runner is the fastest way to make a long table feel festive. Lay it down the middle, set a place for every guest with layered plates and gold flatware, and let the plaid carry the color while the rest stays calm. Red roses, pinecones and a few dried orange slices along the runner are all the extra decoration this table needs.

This is the table to copy for a big family dinner: the pattern hides spills, the runner wipes clean, and every place setting looks the same, so no one feels like they got the plain seat. Add tall taper candles in brass holders for the evening meal.

03 of 30

3. A Red Plaid Runner with Floral Plates

Red plaid and floral china sound like a clash until you see them together. The runner brings the bold Christmas color, the floral plates bring a softer, garden feel, and a sprig of greenery on each plate ties the two together. Crystal glasses and gold cutlery keep the table feeling dressed up rather than busy.

The trick is proportion: the plaid stays in one strip down the middle, and the plates stay in one pattern family. If your plates are busy, keep the napkins plain white and let the runner be the only strong pattern on the table.

04 of 30

4. Bold Red Chargers with Blue Chairs

If your dining chairs are already a color, let them join the scheme. Deep red chargers and gold flatware look striking against blue velvet chairs, and a white tablecloth keeps the whole table from going dark. A small sprig of evergreen on each folded napkin is the only greenery this bold table needs.

This is a good reminder that a christmas table does not have to be red and green on white. Pick the color you already have — blue chairs, a green wall, a wooden table — and build the Christmas palette around it instead of fighting it.

05 of 30

5. A Deep Red Christmas Table with Brass Candlesticks

A deep red tablecloth turns an ordinary dining table into the event itself. Set it with cream plates, gold flatware and tall brass candlesticks, then run a line of greenery, pinecones and red berries down the middle. The candles do the work of a centerpiece, and their light on the red cloth is what makes the whole room glow.

Keep the centerpiece low — greenery flat on the cloth, candles in slender holders — so guests can see each other across the table. A holly sprig tucked into each napkin repeats the greenery at every place without adding another item to buy.

06 of 30

6. Red Velvet Napkins and Gold Chargers

For the most luxurious table on this list, start with texture: a red velvet napkin folded on a gold charger, with a gold-rimmed plate on top. Red goblets and gold flatware carry the palette through the glassware and cutlery, and a garland of greenery and red berries runs down the middle behind it all.

Velvet napkins are the one splurge here, and they are worth it — they look expensive, they photograph beautifully, and they wash and reuse every year. If velvet is out of reach, a plain red cotton napkin on the same gold charger gets you most of the way there.

07 of 30

7. Red Roses on a Gold Runner

Roses are not just for summer. A centerpiece of deep red roses, pinecones and greenery on a glittering gold runner makes a christmas table feel like a celebration rather than a craft project. Tall white tapers in gold holders rise above the flowers, and red napkins with a small name card at each place finish the formal look.

Fresh roses last the evening easily, and the gold runner underneath catches the candlelight and throws it back up onto the flowers. Use a faux gold runner if you want the sparkle without the price — from across the table, no one can tell.

08 of 30

8. Gold Chargers and a Hurricane Candle

A hurricane candle is the easiest centerpiece there is: one big glass, one pillar candle, and a ring of greenery and gold ornaments around its base. Set it on a wooden table with gold chargers and holly-patterned plates, and the whole table looks considered with almost no effort.

The glass does two jobs — it protects the flame from drafts and it makes the candlelight look bigger than it is. This is the centerpiece to choose if you have children or pets at the table, since the flame sits safely behind glass.

09 of 30

9. A Long, Elegant Table in Gold and White

A long table set for a crowd needs a centerpiece that repeats instead of one big arrangement. Run a low garland of greenery and white flowers down the middle, then place gold candle holders and white pillar candles at intervals along it. Every guest gets candlelight nearby, and the table reads as one long, calm line.

Layer the plates — a gold charger, a white dinner plate, a smaller patterned plate on top — and keep the napkins deep red so the table has one strong color against all that white and gold. Crystal glasses catch the candlelight and make the whole setting sparkle.

10 of 30

10. Tall Taper Candles Down a Formal Table

Tall taper candles are the most formal move on this list, and they suit a long white table set for a proper Christmas dinner. Set them in gold holders along a garland of greenery, red berries and gold ornaments, and keep everything else — plates, napkins, flatware — in white and gold so the candles are the height in the room.

The rule for tall candles is simple: keep them above eye level or keep them low, never in between. These tapers clear the sightline easily, so guests can talk across the table while the flames burn well above the plates.

11 of 30

11. Silver Hurricane Holders and Poinsettias

If gold feels too warm for your dining room, silver is the cooler answer. Tall silver hurricane candle holders with pillar candles inside, a garland of greenery and pinecones, and a few red poinsettias give you the same candlelit glow in a silver palette. Gold ornaments tucked into the greenery keep the table from going cold.

Silver and red is a classic pairing that suits modern dining rooms especially well. Add a white tablecloth and let the silver do the reflecting — the candlelight doubles on the metal and the glass.

12 of 30

12. A Bright White Table with Woven Placemats

A bright white dining room with a pale wooden table is the perfect base for a calm christmas table. Woven placemats under white bowls, a greenery garland with white pillar candles down the middle, and a lit tree in the corner do the festive work without a single loud color.

This is the table for a daytime Christmas lunch: it photographs beautifully in daylight, it suits a Scandinavian or minimalist home, and it leaves the room feeling light rather than crowded. Add one red element — a few berries in the garland — if the table feels too quiet.

13 of 30

13. A White Cloth with Red Goblets

A white tablecloth is the most forgiving base you can choose, and red goblets are the one bold note it needs. Set the table with holly-patterned plates, gold flatware and a greenery centerpiece with pinecones, and the red glassware becomes the accent that pulls the whole table together.

Colored glassware is an easy way to change a table year to year without buying new plates: swap red goblets for green ones, or amber ones, and the same white cloth and plates read as a different table entirely.

14 of 30

14. White Pillar Candles in a Gold Tray

A gold tray full of white pillar candles is a centerpiece you can build in five minutes. Set the tray in the middle of the table on a linen runner, surround it with greenery, pinecones and a few silver and gold ornaments, and place a red-bow napkin at every setting. The tray keeps the wax contained and lifts off in one move when the food arrives.

This is the most practical centerpiece on this list: it looks finished, it needs no arranging, and it clears the table faster than any garland. Use LED pillar candles inside the tray if the table will be crowded with reaching hands.

15 of 30

15. A Minimal Scandi Christmas Table in Grey and Green

A Scandinavian christmas table is calm on purpose. A bare pale wood table, grey woven placemats, white plates with a single sprig of evergreen, and a low pine garland with white tapers down the middle — nothing more. The restraint is the decoration.

This look suits a small table best, where a big centerpiece would crowd the plates. Keep the palette to wood, white, grey and green, and let the candlelight be the only warmth. It is also the easiest table on this list to set, because every piece is something you already own.

16 of 30

16. A Simple Linen Table with a Sprig at Each Place

Sometimes the best christmas table is the simplest one: a linen tablecloth, a wooden charger, a white plate and a single sprig of evergreen laid across it. White taper candles in brass holders run down the middle with a thin garland, and nothing competes with the food.

This is the table to copy if you are short on time. The sprig costs nothing — snip it from the garden or the tree — and it makes every guest feel like their place was set on purpose. Fold the napkin under the charger and let the sprig be the decoration.

17 of 30

17. A Rustic Table on Bare Wood

Leave the wooden table bare and let it be the decoration. A linen runner, a wooden charger, layered cream plates and a folded linen napkin are all the place setting needs, and a garland of pine, pinecones and red berries runs down the middle with white pillar candles tucked into it.

Bare wood suits a rustic or farmhouse dining room, and it is the most forgiving surface for a big family meal — no tablecloth to stain, no runner to straighten. A lit tree behind the table and a few candles on the sideboard finish the room without touching the table itself.

18 of 30

18. A Farmhouse Table with Cranberry Bowls

A farmhouse christmas table can be edible as well as decorative. Set each place with layered cream plates and a bowl of fresh cranberries, and run the same berries in glass jars down the middle of the table with greenery, pinecones and white candles. The red of the cranberries is the whole color scheme.

Cranberries are cheap, they last for weeks, and they look like ornaments without the price. After dinner, the bowls go back in the kitchen and the jars become next year's decoration — nothing on this table is single-use.

19 of 30

19. Plaid Placemats and Mason Jars

Plaid placemats, a long weathered wood table and mason jars full of greenery and berries make the most relaxed table on this list. Wooden chargers, patterned plates and a red napkin at each place keep it festive, but nothing here is precious — it is a table built for a long, loud family dinner.

This is the look for a Christmas lunch with children: the placemats wipe clean, the jars do not tip easily, and the candles sit safely in glass. String fairy lights along the garland if the meal runs into the evening.

20 of 30

20. A Wooden Bowl Centerpiece with Pinecones

A wooden dough bowl filled with greenery, pinecones, red berries and pillar candles is the farmhouse centerpiece in its simplest form. Set it on a linen runner with wooden chargers and a burlap-wrapped napkin at each place, and the table feels handmade without a single craft supply.

The bowl is the trick: it contains the whole centerpiece, so it lifts off the table in one move when the serving dishes arrive, and it goes straight onto the sideboard as decoration for the rest of the evening.

21 of 30

21. A Long Wooden Table with Green Napkins

A long wooden table set for a crowd needs a centerpiece that runs the whole length. An evergreen garland laid flat down the middle, with pinecones and a few ornaments tucked into it, does that job for the price of one garland. Green napkins folded on white plates repeat the greenery at every place.

Keep the garland low and the candles in short holders so the table stays open for conversation and passing dishes. This is the table to copy for a big gathering — it scales to any length, and a second garland is all you need for a longer table.

22. Dried Oranges and Amber Glasses by the Fire

Dried orange slices are the oldest Christmas table decoration there is, and they still work. Tuck them into a garland of greenery, pinecones and red berries down the middle of the table, and set the places with red napkins, wooden chargers and amber glasses that glow in the firelight.

The oranges add a warm, spiced scent to the table as well as color, and they cost almost nothing to make — slice, dry in a low oven, and tuck them into the greenery. A fireplace behind the table and a lit tree in the corner complete the room.

23 of 30

23. Twig Wreaths and Dried Orange Slices

For a natural christmas table, skip the ornaments entirely. Small twig wreaths, dried orange slices, red berries, pinecones and fresh greenery laid down the middle of a linen-covered table make a centerpiece that looks gathered rather than bought. A few gold baubles tucked in keep it festive without breaking the natural mood.

Everything on this table can be gathered outside or dried in the kitchen, which makes it the cheapest centerpiece on this list. It also suits a home that leans neutral all year — the table reads as winter, not just Christmas.

24 of 30

24. A Candlelit Table on Dark Wood

A dark wooden table with a white runner, gold candlesticks and red berries is the most atmospheric table on this list. The candlelight reflects off the dark wood and the crystal glasses, and a lit tree behind the table doubles the glow. Keep the plates white and the napkins simple so the light is the decoration.

This is the table for Christmas dinner after dark: turn the overhead lights off, let the candles and the tree do the lighting, and the whole room feels like a restaurant. Use tall tapers in stable holders and keep them clear of the greenery.

25 of 30

25. A Christmas Table Set for Dinner by Candlelight

This is the table as it looks when the meal is actually happening: plates of food on the table, candles lit, glasses filled, and the tree glowing behind it. The centerpiece is a low garland of greenery and red berries with pillar candles in glass holders, and the place settings are simple woven placemats with layered plates.

It is a useful reminder that a christmas table is a working surface, not a display. Leave gaps in the centerpiece for the serving dishes, keep the candles in glass so nothing catches, and set the table so the food can land without moving a decoration.

26 of 30

26. A Family Table with the Tree Behind It

Set the table in front of the tree and let the tree be the backdrop. Green linen napkins, a red runner, gold flatware and a garland of greenery, pinecones and gold ornaments down the middle make a classic family table, and the lit tree behind it does the decorating that the table does not have to.

This is the table for Christmas morning or an early dinner, when the daylight is still coming through the windows. The red runner and green napkins carry the color, and the fireplace and stockings in the background finish the scene without any extra effort.

27 of 30

27. Tree Plates and Fairy Lights

Tree-patterned plates are the most cheerful tableware on this list, and they need very little help. Set them on red chargers with green napkins and gold flatware, run a short garland with fairy lights down the middle, and add red and green taper candles for height. The plates are the pattern, so keep the tablecloth plain white.

Patterned Christmas plates are worth buying once and reusing every year — they make an ordinary Tuesday-night table look like Christmas dinner. If you have them, let them lead and keep everything else quiet.

28 of 30

28. Red Tapers with a Snowflake on Every Napkin

A snowflake ornament hung on a white napkin is the smallest detail on this list and one of the most charming. Set it on a gold-rimmed plate with gold flatware, run red taper candles and a garland of greenery, pinecones and red berries down the middle, and let the fairy lights in the background do the sparkling.

The ornament doubles as a place setting and a small gift — guests can take theirs home. Tie it to the napkin with a red ribbon, and keep the rest of the table white and gold so the red candles and the snowflakes stand out.

29 of 30

29. Poinsettias and a Lantern on a White Table

A white table with poinsettias, a lantern centerpiece and woven placemats is the calmest formal table on this list. Red poinsettias and pinecones bring the Christmas color, white pillar candles bring the light, and a floral runner down the middle keeps the table from feeling stark.

Poinsettias are the easiest live plant to use as decoration — they last the whole season on a windowsill and move to the table for the day. Set the lantern in the middle of the table and the poinsettias at intervals along the runner, and the table is done.

30 of 30

30. A Gold Vase of Roses as the Centerpiece

End with the most elegant centerpiece on this list: a gold vase of red and white roses, pinecones and greenery, with white tapers and gold votives around its base. Set the table with layered plates, gold flatware and a red napkin at each place, and keep the tablecloth white so the flowers are the color in the room.

Fresh flowers on a christmas table feel like a special occasion, and roses hold up well for an evening indoors. Keep the arrangement low enough to see over — a short, full vase beats a tall, thin one on a dinner table every time.

Key Takeaways

  • A christmas table comes down to three moves: a base layer, a low centerpiece guests can see over, and one small detail at every place.
  • Start with the palette — three colors, one metallic — then lay the runner, set the plates and let the candles do the rest.
  • Borrow a runner from one idea here and the napkins from another; the best tables are the mixed ones.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I put on a christmas table?

Start with a base layer, a tablecloth or runner, then a low centerpiece of greenery and candles down the middle. Add a place setting for each guest: charger, dinner plate, napkin, flatware and a glass. Finish with one small detail per place, like a sprig of evergreen or a name card, and keep the middle clear for serving dishes.

How do I make a simple christmas table centerpiece?

Lay a line of fresh or faux greenery straight down the middle of the table and tuck in pinecones, red berries and a few ornaments. Add candles in different heights — pillar candles in glass holders or tapers in brass candlesticks — and leave gaps so serving dishes can land. It takes about ten minutes and needs no arranging skill.

How tall should a christmas table centerpiece be?

Keep it under 12 inches tall, or take it above 24 inches on a slim stand, so guests can see each other across the table. Anything in between sits right at eye level and blocks conversation. A low, long garland with candles is the safest choice on most tables, and it leaves room above for passing dishes.

What colors work best for a christmas table?

Classic red, deep green and gold never fail, and they suit almost any dining room. For a calmer look, try cream with deep green and brass, or white with silver and a little greenery. Pick three colors, repeat them in the runner, napkins and centerpiece, and let one metallic — gold or silver — do the sparkling.

How do I set a christmas table step by step?

Lay the runner first, then set each place from the bottom up: charger, dinner plate, and a smaller salad plate on top. Fork and napkin go on the left, knife on the right with the blade facing in, glass above the knife. Finish with a low centerpiece of greenery and candles down the middle.

How do I decorate a small christmas table?

Use one short garland down the middle instead of a big arrangement, and keep the candles low so the table still feels open. Give each guest a small detail — a sprig on the plate or a folded napkin — rather than a large centerpiece. A table for four needs only a runner, four settings and a line of greenery.

What can I use instead of a tablecloth for a christmas table?

Leave a nice wooden table bare and run a linen or burlap runner down the middle — the wood becomes part of the look. Woven placemats under each plate add warmth without covering the whole table. On a plain table, a kraft-paper runner works too, and children can draw on it before dinner.

When should I set the christmas table?

Set the table the evening before Christmas dinner, so the day itself stays relaxed. Lay the runner, plates and flatware, and build the centerpiece — but light the candles only when guests sit down. If the table is also your everyday table, set it after lunch and keep the centerpiece low so the room stays usable.
