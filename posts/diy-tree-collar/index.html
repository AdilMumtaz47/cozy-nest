30 DIY Tree Collar Ideas for a Finished Christmas Tree

2026-11-26

Save this list on Pinterest

A diy tree collar is the neatest way to finish a Christmas tree: a short ring that hides the stand, the cords and the water reservoir, so the tree looks complete from the floor up. Collars come in every material now — galvanized metal, woven wicker, rattan, burlap and soft fabric — and each one changes the mood of the tree underneath it.

This list gathers 30 collar ideas in mixed materials, with no wooden collars at all — wood gets its own list. Whether your room is farmhouse, glamorous or minimalist, there is a collar here that fits it, and most of them need no building at all.

1. Galvanized Snowflake Cutout Collar

A galvanized metal collar with snowflake cutouts is the farmhouse classic. The cutouts let the tree lights shine through in snowflake shapes, and the dull silver metal sits comfortably next to plaid, burlap and red ornaments.

Galvanized collars usually fold flat for storage and clip together in seconds, which makes them the easiest option on this list. Wipe the metal with a dry cloth before storing so it never rusts.

💡 Quick Tip: Galvanized collars usually fold flat and clip together in seconds — the easiest option here.

2. Christmas Tree Farm Galvanized Collar

A printed Christmas Tree Farm design turns a plain metal collar into a piece of signage, like the signs at a tree lot. The red lettering and little pine trees do the decorating, so the tree above it can stay simple.

This is a bought collar, not a build, so the DIY part is the styling: pair it with a plaid tree skirt underneath or a few wrapped gifts beside it, and let the print be the only text in the room.

4. Red Truck Merry Little Christmas Collar

The little red truck carrying a tree is the most nostalgic print a collar can wear, and on galvanized metal it looks like an old-fashioned sign. The Merry Little Christmas lettering makes the whole base of the tree feel like a greeting card.

Keep the rest of the base simple when the collar carries a print — no extra signage nearby, or the two messages compete. Red and white ornaments above it pick up the colors in the print.

💡 Quick Tip: Keep the rest of the base simple when the collar carries a print, or the messages compete.

5. Gold Star Band Galvanized Collar

A gold band with white stars around a galvanized collar adds just enough glamour to lift the metal out of the farmhouse and into something dressier. The stars echo the star on top of the tree, which ties the whole tree together.

This collar suits trees with gold and silver ornaments and warm white lights. A gold ribbon on the gifts underneath it repeats the band and makes the whole base feel planned.

6. Silver Collar with Gold Rim

A silver metal collar with a thin gold rim is the most elegant metal option here. The gold edge catches the light and frames the base of the tree like a picture frame, and the whole collar reads as jewelry for the tree.

Pair it with metallic ornaments and a gold star topper, and keep the tree skirt out entirely — the collar is doing that job. A plain floor underneath keeps the focus on the metal.

7. Silver Textured Metal Collar

A silver metal collar with a hammered, textured surface catches the tree lights in a hundred tiny highlights, and it looks far more expensive than a flat metal collar. Under a lit tree it glows softly, and the texture hides fingerprints and scuffs that a smooth finish would show.

This collar suits a tree with silver and white ornaments and warm white lights, especially a flocked tree. The textured surface also grips the floor slightly, so the collar stays put when someone brushes past it.

💡 Quick Tip: Textured metal hides fingerprints and scuffs — and it grips the floor slightly, so the collar stays put.

8. Gold Hammered Metal Collar

A hammered gold collar brings warmth and texture that flat metal cannot match. The dimpled surface catches the tree lights in a hundred tiny highlights, and the gold suits a tree with warm white lights and amber ornaments.

Hammered metal shows fingerprints, so handle it by the rim when you set it up. A quick wipe with a soft cloth before guests arrive restores the shine in seconds.

A collar made from distressed white tin ceiling panels looks like it was salvaged from an old farmhouse, even when it is brand new. The chipped paint and embossed pattern give it more character than any flat metal collar.

This is a good choice for a shabby-chic or cottage room, especially with a white or flocked tree. The pale metal reflects the lights, so the base of the tree glows softly in the evening.

10. Copper Slatted Collar

Copper slats give a tree collar a warm, modern glow that silver metal never achieves. The vertical slats echo the shape of the tree above them, and the copper deepens in color as it ages, which only improves the look.

Copper suits rooms with warm wood floors and brass or gold ornaments. Polish it once a year if you like it bright, or let it develop a natural patina for a softer, older look.

💡 Quick Tip: Polish copper once a year if you like it bright, or let it develop a natural patina.

11. Woven Seagrass Basket Collar

A woven seagrass basket collar brings natural texture to the base of the tree, and it is the softest-looking option on this list. The open weave lets a little light through, and the honey color warms up a room with white walls.

Seagrass collars are usually sold as baskets, so measure the opening against your stand before you buy — the tree needs to fit through the top. A basket collar also hides the water reservoir completely, which is a bonus with a real tree.

12. Dark Wicker Basket Collar

A dark wicker collar is the most dramatic basket option, and it suits a tree with deep red ornaments and a dark wood floor. The dark weave reads as solid and substantial, so the tree looks anchored rather than perched.

Dark wicker shows dust, so give it a shake outside before you set it up and a quick vacuum with a brush attachment once a week. Keep it away from the fireplace — wicker dries out and becomes brittle near heat.

13. Light Wicker Scalloped Collar

A light wicker collar with a scalloped top edge is the prettiest basket on this list. The scallops soften the hard line where the collar meets the tree, and the pale weave keeps the base of the tree feeling light and airy.

This collar suits a slim tree in a bright room, especially with white or pastel ornaments. The scalloped edge is the feature, so keep the lowest branches trimmed just above it.

💡 Quick Tip: Keep the lowest branches trimmed just above the scalloped edge so the scallops show.

14. Light Rattan Collar

A light rattan collar is wicker's sleeker cousin: same natural weave, but with a cleaner, more regular pattern. It suits modern and coastal rooms where a rough basket would look out of place, and it pairs beautifully with a white tree.

Rattan is light and easy to move, which makes watering a real tree simpler — lift the collar off, water, and set it back. Store it in a dry place, since damp rattan can mildew over the summer.

15. Gray-Brown Wicker Collar

A gray-brown wicker collar is the most versatile basket color: it works with red, gold, silver and white ornaments alike, and it never clashes with a wood floor. The grey tones keep it from feeling too yellow, which plain wicker sometimes does.

This is the basket to choose if you redecorate the tree every year, since it goes with every color scheme. A grey-brown collar also hides dust and needle drop better than a pale one.

16. Honey Wicker Collar

A honey-colored wicker collar is the warmest basket on this list, and it glows under warm white lights. The golden weave suits a traditional tree with red and gold ornaments, and it brings a honeyed warmth to a room with cool white walls.

Honey wicker darkens slightly with age and sunlight, so store it out of direct sun. A coat of clear matte sealer once a year keeps the color even and the weave from drying out.

💡 Quick Tip: Seal honey wicker once a year with clear matte sealer to keep the color even.

Keep this collar away from the fireplace and radiators, since the pale finish shows soot and scorch marks. A quick brush with a dry cloth once a week keeps the weave looking fresh.

Faux fur sheds a little at first, so shake it out outside before you set it up. It is also the warmest collar to handle, which matters when you are crawling under the tree to water it in December.

19. White Chunky Knit Collar

A white chunky knit collar looks like a giant sweater for the tree stand, and it brings instant coziness to the base of the tree. The thick knit texture catches the light beautifully, and it suits a small tree in a bedroom or a reading nook.

Knit collars are usually a cover that slips over a frame, so check that the frame fits your stand. Hand wash it gently after Christmas if it has picked up tree sap, and dry it flat so it keeps its shape.

💡 Quick Tip: Hand wash gently after Christmas if it picks up sap, and dry flat so it keeps its shape.

20. Red Plaid Fabric Collar

A red plaid fabric collar is the coziest option here, and it is the easiest to make yourself: a cone of plaid fabric over a cardboard or wire frame. The plaid echoes blankets and scarves, so the whole tree feels like a winter cabin.

Choose a heavy cotton or flannel so the cone holds its shape, and line it with a stiff interfacing if the fabric is light. A plaid collar is also the easiest to store — fold it flat and it takes up no space at all.

Pleating takes patience, so buy a ready-made cone if you are short on time and add your own trim. A cream fabric shows dust, so keep it away from the fireplace and vacuum it gently with a brush attachment.

Brown fabric hides dust and needle drop better than any pale collar, which makes it a practical choice for a real tree. A cone in a heavy linen or canvas holds its shape all season without sagging.

White shows every scuff, so handle it with clean hands and store it in a bag. The payoff is a base that looks clean and intentional, like the tree was designed rather than decorated.

24. Burlap Drawstring Collar

A burlap drawstring collar is the simplest fabric option: a sack of burlap gathered around the stand with a twine tie. It takes five minutes to make, costs almost nothing, and looks wonderfully rustic under a farmhouse tree.

Use a double layer of burlap so the stand does not show through, and tie the drawstring in a bow at the front. Burlap frays, so finish the top edge with a simple hem or a row of stitching.

Sew the tassels to a cord and tie the cord around the collar, so you can remove them for washing. Gold tassels suit a tree with gold ornaments and warm white lights best.

27. Ruffled Burlap Collar

A ruffled burlap collar is burlap dressed up: layers of gathered fabric that look like a skirt for the tree. It is the most feminine fabric option here, and it suits a tree with soft colors and vintage ornaments.

Gather the burlap with a running stitch and pin the ruffles to a base cone, spacing them evenly. Three layers is the sweet spot — more than that starts to look heavy, and fewer looks unfinished.

28. Burlap Sack with Gingham Bow

A burlap sack tied with a big gingham bow is the farmhouse classic in its simplest form. The bow is the whole decoration, and it takes thirty seconds to tie, which makes this the fastest collar on the list to refresh each year.

Choose a gingham that matches your ornaments — black and white for a modern farmhouse, red and white for a traditional one. A wired ribbon holds its shape better than a flat one.

Takeaway

A tree collar does not have to be wood to look finished — metal, wicker, rattan, burlap and fabric each bring a different mood to the same tree, and most of them need no building at all. Pick the material that matches your room, check that it fits your stand, and let the collar do the quiet work of hiding the stand while the tree takes the applause. Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Christmas tree collar?

A tree collar is a short ring or box, usually 12 to 14 inches tall, that sits around the base of a Christmas tree and hides the stand, the cords and the water reservoir. It replaces a tree skirt, looks neater than a skirt, and works with any tree. Collars come in metal, wicker, rattan, wood, burlap and fabric.

What size tree collar do I need?

Measure the widest part of your tree stand and add at least two inches on every side. Most artificial trees need a collar 20 to 24 inches wide, while small tabletop trees need one 12 to 15 inches wide. Too small will not fit over the stand, and too big swallows the lowest branches.

Are metal tree collars better than wicker ones?

Neither is better — they suit different rooms. Metal collars are sturdier, easier to clean and fold flat for storage, and they suit farmhouse and modern rooms. Wicker and rattan collars are lighter and warmer looking, and they suit coastal, boho and Scandinavian rooms. Choose by the room, not by the material.

Can I make a tree collar myself?

Yes, and the easiest ones take minutes. A burlap sack tied with twine is a collar, as is a cone of plaid fabric over a cardboard frame. A galvanized metal collar is best bought, since cutting sheet metal at home is hard. Wicker and rattan collars are also best bought, then styled to match your tree.

How do I water a real tree with a collar on?

Choose a collar with an open bottom and set the stand on a waterproof tray inside it. Leave a gap at the back for the watering can, or use a long-spout can to reach the reservoir over the rim. Check the water level every day — a real tree drinks a surprising amount in a warm room.

Do tree collars work with flocked trees?

Yes, and they look wonderful together. A white or pale collar blends into the flocking, while a dark metal or wicker collar gives a flocked tree a strong base to sit on. Shake the tree outside before you set the collar, since flocking sheds, and wipe a pale collar after setup.

How do I store a tree collar after Christmas?

Metal collars usually fold flat — wipe them dry first so they never rust. Wicker and rattan collars stack or nest, and they need a dry place so they do not mildew. Fabric and burlap collars fold flat in a bag. Keep every collar out of direct sun and away from damp garages.

Can I use a tree collar with a tree skirt too?

You can, but you rarely need to. A collar replaces a skirt, and using both usually looks crowded. The one exception is a fur or faux-fur rug under a metal or wicker collar, which softens the base and catches fallen needles. If you use both, keep the skirt flat and simple so the collar stays the feature.
